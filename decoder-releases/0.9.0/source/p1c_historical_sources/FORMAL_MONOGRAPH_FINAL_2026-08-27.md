## Abstract

Infinity Grid algebra studies a precise finite question. Suppose a recursively composed microscopic object has an interior that will never again be inspected directly. What must remain at its boundary so that every later lawful composition behaves exactly as if the interior were still present?

The frozen finite algebra begins from a primitive constructor, J3. Every exposed port carries a supply/need pair `(P,M)`. A complete boundary interface also carries a finite label `L`, a weakest-link score `s`, destination data `T`, and a Boolean `stay`. Two selected ports may bridge only when each side's need is either empty or met by the other side's supply. Composition consumes the selected ports, retains all others, joins labels, takes the minimum score, transports declared destination data, and conjoins the stay flags. Boundary objects are compared up to anonymous-port isomorphism, not by serialization order or construction provenance.

The central closure theorem states that the recursively constructed macro relation `T_X` equals the true behavioral boundary relation `B(X)` for every finite object generated by the audited primitive and compositor. # PART 0 - BEFORE THE ALGEBRA: ONTOLOGICAL MOTIVATION

## 1. The question before mathematics

Infinity Grid did not begin with a group, a category, a lattice equation, or a desired physical law. It began with a conceptual discomfort: the ordinary phrase *something rather than nothing* treats nothing as though it were an alternative condition, perhaps an empty region or a prior state. But the moment nothing is given a location, a duration, a boundary, a possibility, or a relation to something, it has already been given structure. It is no longer absolute nothing.

This is the philosophical foundation of the program. It is intentionally placed before the mathematics because it motivates the kind of formal problem we ask. It is not offered as a mathematical proof that J3 is uniquely correct, nor as an empirical observation. It is a constraint on what a foundational account should not silently assume.

The guiding questions are:

- Can absolute nothing coherently coexist with anything?
- Can existence begin if the alleged prior condition has no structure, law, time, possibility, or carrier?
- Can a foundational existent be externally bounded if there is literally nothing outside it?
- Can a completely undifferentiated One produce higher-order structure without admitting internal distinction?
- Can we construct a formal system whose higher-order organization is discovered rather than repeatedly inserted?

The algebra in this book is one disciplined attempt to turn those questions into a finite research program.

> **Plain-language reading.** The project starts one step earlier than most mathematical physics. Before asking what equations the universe follows, it asks whether the usual picture of a universe appearing inside or beside “nothing” is even coherent.

### Status

**Philosophical premise.** None of the claims in Part 0 is a theorem of the finite algebra. A reader may reject the ontology and still evaluate every formal theorem and audit in the remainder of the book.

## 2. Why absolute nothing cannot sit beside something

Suppose there is something, and beside it there is nothing. The word *beside* has already introduced a relation. There is now a distinction between a region or condition occupied by something and another region or condition occupied by nothing. The nothing has an interface: on one side, nothing; on the other, something.

But a boundary is structure. A relation is structure. A side is structure. A distinction is structure. Therefore the alleged nothing is not absolute nothing. It is better described as empty space, an unoccupied state, a vacuum, a null value, or an absent object within a larger formal setting. All of those may be physically or mathematically meaningful, but none is absolute nothing.

The argument can be stated compactly:

1. Absolute nothing has no properties, relations, boundaries, possibilities, or internal distinctions.
2. If nothing coexists with something, the two are related by coexistence and distinguished by a boundary or contrast.
3. The alleged nothing then has relational structure.
4. Therefore it is not absolute nothing.

This does not prove that every philosopher must use the word *nothing* in this way. It fixes the meaning used by Infinity Grid: **absolute nothing means the absence of all structure, not merely an empty state inside a structure.**

The consequence is not that “nothing is a strange substance.” It is the opposite: nothing cannot serve as a substance, container, backdrop, cause, prior state, or exterior.

### A useful distinction

| Term | Meaning in this document |
|---|---|
| Empty state | A state inside a formal system in which no ordinary object is present. |
| Vacuum | A lawful physical or algebraic state with definite structure and possible behavior. |
| Null value | A distinguished value in a language or algebra. |
| Absolute nothing | No system, state, relation, law, boundary, possibility, or value at all. |

Only the last is rejected as a coherent coexisting or preceding alternative to existence.

## 3. Eternity and unboundedness without assuming time or geometry

If existence began, there would be a distinction between a condition in which existence was absent and a condition in which it was present. A transition would have occurred. Yet a transition requires at least enough structure to distinguish before from after, to carry a rule or possibility of change, and to make the transition meaningful. Absolute nothing supplies none of this.

Infinity Grid therefore takes existence to be **eternal in an ontological sense**: it has no creation boundary from absolute nothing. This must not be confused with saying that the foundation has existed for an infinite number of seconds. Seconds, clocks, and even ordered physical time have not yet been earned. The claim is weaker and more basic: there is no external transition from nonbeing into being.

The same caution applies to infinity. If the foundational existent had an external boundary, there would be something on one side and an outside on the other. If the outside is absolute nothing, the border argument returns. Infinity Grid therefore begins with **unboundedness in an ontological sense**: no external container or exterior is presupposed.

This is not yet an infinite Euclidean space, an infinite cubic lattice, or an infinite metric manifold. Geometry has not been earned. The initial word *unbounded* means only that the foundational existent is not bordered by absolute nothing.

> **Plain-language reading.** “Eternal” here does not mean an endlessly old object sitting in a pre-existing clock. “Infinite” does not mean a huge three-dimensional room. Both words initially mean only: no birth from nothing and no outside made of nothing.

### Why this wording matters

Earlier formulations of Infinity Grid sometimes moved too quickly from the ontological argument to a spatially infinite lattice. The present algebra branch is more disciplined. It keeps the logical constraint while withholding geometric interpretation. A discrete set of formal instances may be useful for the algebra, but the ontology alone does not prove a lattice embedding, a dimension, a metric, or locality in the physical sense.

## 4. Internal distinction: why a featureless One cannot build higher order

Rejecting an external boundary appears to leave a single unbounded existent. But a completely featureless and indivisible One cannot support higher-order structure. If nothing within it can be distinguished from anything else, then there are no instances, no interfaces, no alternatives, no relations, and no compositions. It may be ontologically sufficient for bare existence, but it is structurally sterile.

To account for a world containing differentiated higher-order structures, the foundation must admit **internal distinction without an external divider**. This is the conceptual doorway to a plurality of formal instances.

The reasoning is:

1. Higher-order structure requires distinguishable constituents or roles.
2. Constituents must be relatable in more than one lawful way.
3. Relations must be composable if larger structures are to arise.
4. If the foundation is all that exists, these distinctions and relations must be internal to it.
5. Therefore the foundational formalism must allow internally distinguishable instances and lawful composition without appealing to an outside constructor.

This does not logically force the particular J3 primitive. It does not prove that the instances are physical particles, points in space, or moments in time. It establishes the research demand: find a minimal internally composable algebra and observe what it can build.

### One entity, many occurrences

The phrase *one unbounded entity* need not mean one featureless token. It can mean one ontological whole with many internally distinguishable occurrences. The finite algebra represents this possibility through anonymous instances with interfaces. Their identity is not a coordinate or permanent name. It is the structure of their lawful boundary behavior.

This separation later becomes important. Anonymous ports are real interface distinctions, but their serialization labels are not physical content. Distinct mature nodes in the inter-node program are separate algebraic instances, but they still have no location or distance.

## 5. From logical constraint to a frozen formal experiment

The philosophical argument does not hand us a ready-made algebra. A formal choice must still be made. Infinity Grid therefore proceeds in two stages.

### Stage 1: specify a minimal finite formal world

The project fixes:

- a primitive constructor;
- finite boundary data;
- a local bridge predicate;
- a deterministic output constructor;
- an equivalence relation identifying anonymous-interface relabelings;
- a weakest-link selection rule applied only after the complete relation is formed.

These are human choices. Scientific honesty requires saying so plainly. The base algebra was designed and then frozen; it was not dictated word-for-word by the ontology.

### Stage 2: stop designing the higher-order answer

Once the primitive and compositor were frozen, the research method changed. The task was no longer to invent a desired hierarchy. It was to enumerate lawful possibilities, compose them exactly, compare them under behaviorally justified equivalence, formulate hypotheses about emerging organization, and attempt to kill those hypotheses.

A compact methodological statement is:

> **The foundational rules were specified once; the higher-order organization was not. From that point onward, the task was not to design the desired structure but to expose the space of lawful consequences and ask which descriptions survived exact substitution, adversarial context, and recursive composition.**

This is what gives the program its experimental character. The “experiment” is mathematical and computational rather than physical. The algebra supplies the possibility space; the researcher observes its exact consequences.

### The standing workflow

1. Freeze the question before opening the result.
2. Reuse theorems and earlier exact artifacts before generating new data.
3. Build the complete lawful relation before selection.
4. Separate discovery instruments from interpretation instruments.
5. Enforce a Local Sufficiency Gate before invoking higher-order explanations.
6. Preserve negative results and retractions.
7. Package exact code, inputs, outputs, runtime, checksums, and replay instructions.
8. Stop rather than invent an unearned operation.

The last rule has repeatedly mattered. When a cycle could not be erased by the old binary compositor, the program did not invent self-contraction. When actualization was underdetermined, it did not insert probability or a selector. When holonomy was zero-or-undefined, it did not call the result curvature.

## 6. What the ontology does not prove

The ontological motivation constrains the starting question, but it does not establish the later science by itself.

It does **not** prove:

- that J3 is the unique primitive;
- that the frozen bridge law is the only possible law;
- that the finite algebra describes nature;
- that mature nodes are physical entities;
- that the inter-node carrier is spacetime;
- that dimension, geometry, fields, matter, or cosmology follow;
- that one actual universe must be selected;
- that an unbounded ontology must be represented by a geometric infinity.

What it contributes is a disciplined refusal to rely on an external creator, an outside container, or an unexplained transition from absolute nothing. It motivates a self-contained compositional search. The rest of the book must stand on formal definitions, proofs, exact audits, and explicit limitations.

> **Plain-language reading.** The philosophy tells us why we looked for a self-contained algebra. It does not let us skip the algebra. Every interesting structure after that has to be earned.

# PART I - THE FINITE RECURSIVE ALGEBRA

## 7. The primitive finite world

The finite algebra begins with a primitive object called **J3**. The name is historical; what matters here is its role. J3 supplies a finite, exhaustively enumerable microscopic world from which all later finite composites are generated.

A primitive has a finite collection of local event choices. The canonical constructor:

1. enumerates every possible local event-index combination in its declared domain;
2. rejects combinations that violate the primitive legality rules;
3. projects each lawful microscopic realization to its declared boundary behavior;
4. canonicalizes that boundary modulo the declared interface equivalence;
5. retains the complete set of lawful boundary records.

This primitive exhaustiveness is the base of the recursive proof. If a lawful primitive state were discarded at the start, no later macro constructor could recover it.

### Primitive exactness

Let `Mic(P)` be the finite set of lawful microscopic realizations of primitive `P`. Let `B(x)` be the declared behavioral boundary of realization `x`. Then the primitive relation is

```text
T_P = { [B(x)] : x in Mic(P) }
```

where brackets denote the anonymous-interface isomorphism class.

**Audited premise.** The J3 constructor enumerates the declared primitive domain exhaustively and projects every lawful realization to the complete declared boundary fields.

**Theorem base case.** Given primitive exactness, `T_P = B(P)` for every primitive `P` in the audited constructor.

> **Plain-language reading.** The base objects are not sampled. Every legal primitive possibility is kept. Later proofs would be meaningless if the first layer had already thrown possibilities away.

### A finite world, not yet a physical world

The primitive objects are formal instances. A primitive is not yet a point in space, a particle, a moment, or a field value. The algebra knows only its legal microscopic choices and its exposed behavioral interface. Physical interpretation is deliberately postponed.

## 8. Boundary interfaces and anonymous ports

A microscopic composite may contain a large interior. Future objects are not allowed to inspect that interior directly. They encounter only the composite's exposed boundary.

The final boundary object is

```text
B = (finite port set, port -> (P,M), L, s, T, stay) / isomorphism
```

The fields have the following roles.

| Field | Behavioral role |
|---|---|
| Exposed port set | Interfaces through which future legal attachments may occur. |
| `P` | Supply data carried by one port. |
| `M` | Need data carried by one port. |
| `L` | A finite label combined by join. |
| `s` | Weakest-link score. |
| `T` | Declared destination structure used by later output construction. |
| `stay` | Boolean field combined by conjunction. |

In code, the ports must be stored somehow. They may therefore appear as an ordered tuple. But the order of that tuple is not part of the mathematical object. A permutation of anonymous port labels does not create a new behavior, provided the complete structure - including the corresponding `T` entries - is transported consistently.

### Why anonymous ports matter

Suppose two disjoint attachments are made in opposite orders. A serializer may list the retained port blocks in construction order. The raw tuples can then differ even when each port, supply/need pair, and destination entry has an exact structural counterpart. Literal tuple equality would mistake history-dependent bookkeeping for behavior.

The corrected equivalence is:

```text
(r * z1) * z2  ~=  (r * z2) * z1
```

where `~=` denotes structure-preserving boundary-interface isomorphism.

This correction was not cosmetic. The symbolic interchange audit found raw tuple inequality in all 5,376 tagged test cases, while every case became equal after the declared origin-consistent alignment. The test showed two things at once:

- construction-order serialization was real;
- behavioral asymmetry was not.

### No provenance variable

A tempting repair would have been to add a permanent field recording which internal object each port came from. The algebra rejected that move. Construction provenance is not needed for future behavior once the complete boundary interface is present. The right repair is to type the boundary correctly - as an anonymous structured interface - rather than enlarging the state with hidden history.

**Theorem/definition consequence.** Port labels are interface handles, not physical or behavioral identities.

> **Plain-language reading.** Two boxes can have their sockets printed in different orders and still be the same box. The outside world cares which kind of socket connects to which declared destination structure, not which one happened to be written first in a file.

## 9. The bridge law

A composition begins by selecting one exposed port from each of two boundary objects. Only the selected ports' supply and need data may decide whether the bridge is lawful.

For selected ports `A` and `B`:

```text
Bridge(A,B) iff
  (M_A = 0 or M_A intersect P_B is nonempty)
  and
  (M_B = 0 or M_B intersect P_A is nonempty).
```

The predicate is symmetric under swapping the endpoints.

### What the bridge does not read

Bridge acceptance does not consult:

- the global label `L`;
- the score `s`;
- destination structure `T`;
- `stay`;
- the identity of a microscopic representative;
- hidden ancestry or path provenance;
- unused ports;
- construction level;
- position, distance, direction, or geometry.

This locality is one of the most important closure premises. If the bridge consulted hidden microscopic information, a macro boundary could not safely replace its interior.

### Bridge Sufficiency

If two boundary interfaces are isomorphic and the selected attachment port is transported through the same interface isomorphism, then bridge acceptance against the same external port is identical.

**Proof sketch.** The isomorphism preserves the selected port's `(P,M)` data. The bridge predicate reads only those data. Therefore its truth value is unchanged.

This small statement is the first link in the full substitution theorem.

### Neutral and selective channels

A need value `M=0` makes one side permissive: it does not demand a matching supply from the other side. Later, in the mature-node alphabet, most lawful port-type pairs are ordinary `M=0/M=0` possibilities. Only a few port types create genuinely selective channel families. That later structure is inherited from this same frozen bridge law; no new mature-node compatibility rule is added.

## 10. Composition, weakest link, and complete relations

When a lawful bridge is made, the two selected ports become internal. Every other exposed port remains exposed. If an `n`-port object is joined to an `m`-port object by one bridge, the result has

```text
n + m - 2
```

exposed ports.

The output record is constructed only from the two input boundary records and the selected incidence:

```text
L_out    = join(L_1, L_2)
s_out    = min(s_1, s_2)
ports    = all unconsumed exposed ports, copied by value
T_out    = constructed only from stored T data
stay_out = stay_1 AND stay_2
```

No microscopic parent identity is consulted.

### Output Sufficiency

If two accepted input pairs are related by the declared interface isomorphisms and the selected ports correspond, then the resulting output boundaries are isomorphic.

The statement is sometimes called **Output Sufficiency** and sometimes **Output Equivariance**. Its content is simple: once the declared boundary records are complete, the output constructor has no reason and no permission to reopen the hidden interiors.

### The weakest-link score

The score of a composite is the minimum of the scores carried by its two parents:

```text
s_out = min(s_1, s_2).
```

A composite can therefore never be stronger than its weakest constituent. This rule propagates recursively.

### The additive rank

If `p(X)` is the number of exposed ports, define

```text
nu(X) = p(X) - 2.
```

Because one bridge consumes exactly two exposed ports,

```text
nu(X * Y)
  = (p(X) + p(Y) - 2) - 2
  = (p(X)-2) + (p(Y)-2)
  = nu(X) + nu(Y).
```

Thus `nu` is a graded, well-founded additive rank for the finite generated algebra. This rank is an algebraic construction measure, not physical time.

### Why complete relations come before selection

A composite does not generally have one lawful boundary record. Different microscopic realizations and attachment choices may produce different lawful outcomes. The algebra must first construct the complete relation of all lawful boundary behaviors. Only afterward may it apply weakest-link maximization.

Premature selection is dangerous. A locally nonmaximal record may be necessary for a later context or may tie after additional weakest-link constraints. Therefore the canonical discipline is:

```text
construct all lawful outputs -> canonicalize exact duplicates -> then select all tied maxima.
```

No arbitrary winner is chosen among ties.

## 11. Exact macro relations and selection

For a finite composite `X`, define its true behavioral boundary relation:

```text
B(X) = { [B(x)] : x is a lawful microscopic realization of X }.
```

The recursively constructed macro relation is `T_X`. For a binary composite:

```text
T_(X*Y) = {
  [Out(r,z)] :
  r in T_X,
  z in T_Y,
  Bridge(r,z)
}.
```

The constructor forms every lawful compatible pair, applies the output law, and deduplicates only outputs in the same boundary-interface isomorphism class.

### Selection

For a complete relation `R`, define

```text
Sel(R) = { r in R : s(r) = max score in R }.
```

Selection retains the entire tied-max subset.

### Exact substitutability as the goal

The algebra is not trying merely to compress data. It asks whether the hidden interior can be forgotten without changing any future lawful behavior. The target statement is:

```text
T_X = B(X)
```

up to the declared anonymous-interface equivalence.

If this equality holds, any future finite context can use `T_X` instead of reopening the microscopic object `X`.

> **Plain-language reading.** The macro is not a rough summary. It is the complete outside behavior of the hidden object. If it is exact, no legal future experiment at the boundary can tell whether the box contains the original microscopic construction or only its macro relation.

# PART II - CLOSURE AND EXACT SUBSTITUTION

## 12. The proof chain

The closure result is not one enormous theorem. It is a chain of small statements, each blocking a different way hidden information could leak back into future behavior.

| Result | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Primitive Exactness | Every lawful primitive boundary is present. |
| Bridge Sufficiency | Corresponding isomorphic selected ports give the same accept/reject decision. |
| Output Sufficiency / Equivariance | Accepted isomorphic inputs produce isomorphic outputs. |
| One-Step Quotient Stability | One legal composition cannot distinguish isomorphic boundaries. |
| Boundary Closure | A legal composition produces another valid boundary object of the same algebraic kind. |
| Finite-Context Quotient Stability | No finite branching composition context can distinguish isomorphic boundaries. |
| Selection Congruence | Equal full relations have equal maximal score and equal complete tied-max subsets. |
| Bijection Independence | The output class does not depend on which valid witnessing isomorphism is chosen. |
| Recursive Constructor Completeness | `T_X` equals `B(X)` for every finite generated object. |

### One-Step Quotient Stability

Let `r ~= r'` be isomorphic boundary records. Attach each to the same external record through corresponding ports. Bridge Sufficiency gives the same legality decision. If the bridge is lawful, Output Equivariance gives isomorphic outputs. Therefore one composition step respects the quotient.

### Boundary Closure

Composition consumes two selected ports, retains finite residual ports, and computes `L`, `s`, `T`, and `stay` by the declared closed operations. The result is again a finite boundary interface of the same kind. No new field type is created by composition.

### Bijection Independence

A symmetric boundary may admit more than one witnessing isomorphism. The output class must not depend on which witness is used to align the ports. This was established deductively from equivariance and reverified on a real symmetric `T_ABC` core with eight automorphisms: distinct witnessing bijections led to the same output classes.

## 13. Recursive completeness

The central theorem is proved by structural induction on the generated object.

### Theorem: Recursive Constructor Completeness

For every finite object `X` generated by the audited primitive and binary single-bridge compositor,

```text
T_X = B(X)
```

as sets of anonymous-interface isomorphism classes.

### Base case

For a primitive `P`, primitive exhaustiveness gives exactly the boundary classes of every lawful microscopic realization:

```text
T_P = B(P).
```

### Inductive step

Assume

```text
T_X = B(X)
T_Y = B(Y).
```

We must prove equality for `X*Y`.

#### Soundness

Take any macro output in `T_(X*Y)`. By construction, it comes from records `r in T_X` and `z in T_Y` that pass Bridge.

By the inductive hypothesis, `r` and `z` have lawful microscopic witnesses `x` and `y`. Bridge Sufficiency ensures that the corresponding microscopic selected ports also pass the bridge law. Output Sufficiency then says the microscopic composite has exactly the macro output boundary class. Therefore the macro constructor invents no behavior.

#### Completeness

Take any lawful microscopic realization of `X*Y`. It has lawful microscopic parents `x` and `y` joined through a lawful selected bridge. By the inductive hypothesis, their boundary classes occur in `T_X` and `T_Y`. The macro constructor considers this compatible pair. Output Sufficiency reproduces the microscopic boundary class. Therefore the macro constructor omits no behavior.

Soundness and completeness give equality.

### Why the proof is finite but general

The induction applies to every finite generated composition tree. It does not extrapolate from a large sample. The theorem's scope is infinite in the sense that it covers arbitrarily large finite generated objects, provided the audited primitive and compositor semantics remain unchanged.

## 14. Finite contexts, macro-macro composition, and selection congruence

### Finite-context theorem

Let `C[-]` be any finite legal composition context containing one hole. The context may branch; a later attachment may use a port introduced by an earlier attachment. If

```text
B_X ~= B_Y,
```

then

```text
C[B_X] ~= C[B_Y].
```

### Proof idea

Follow the unique subtree containing the hole. At the first composition above the hole, One-Step Quotient Stability preserves isomorphism. Repeat upward through the finite tree. Branches outside the hole-containing subtree are identical in both contexts. Structural induction reaches the root.

This is stronger than a chain-only substitution statement. It covers arbitrary finite branching syntax.

### Macro-macro closure

Because every exact macro relation equals its microscopic behavioral relation, two macros may be composed directly. There is no requirement that one side remain microscopic. Later audits compared macro-macro composition against exact microscopic six-object constructions, including hostile saturation and score-obstruction families, and found exact equality in the declared quotient.

### Selection Congruence

If two complete relations are equal up to the declared interface equivalence, their score multisets are identical. Therefore their maximal scores are identical, and the complete subsets attaining that maximum are identical.

Thus exact substitution survives the final weakest-link selection:

```text
T_X = B(X)
  implies
Sel(C[T_X]) ~= Sel(C[X]).
```

### Exact hiding of an interior

The closure theorem provides an algebraic notion of encapsulation. A finite interior can be hidden behind its complete boundary relation without changing any future finite behavior visible to the compositor.

This makes hierarchical construction possible in principle. Once one level has been reduced exactly, higher levels need not reopen its microscopic carrier.

The closed result is intentionally implementation-scoped. It applies to:

The words port, bridge, overlap, interval, cycle, and topology are algebraic. They do not by themselves license distance, dimension, space, causal propagation, or a metric.

## 16A. Why formal integration changes the presentation

The original proof programme correctly discovered that anonymous interface structure, rather than construction order or provenance, is the relevant behavioral carrier. External mathematics now lets us state the generic part in a standard compositional language and isolate the part that is specifically IG.

The goal is not to rename IG as a PROP, Petri net, operad, or category. Each of those names brings operations and theorems that may exceed the frozen syntax. The correct procedure is:

1. define the exact carrier independently of code serialization;
2. identify the smallest established compositional envelope that contains the frozen operations;
3. prove the embedding and its hypotheses;
4. import only the resulting generic consequences;
5. leave J3-specific completeness and generated organization visible as the actual IG residue.

**Plain-language reading.** We now know which pieces are ordinary mathematics of plugging finite boxes together, and which pieces depend on the particular IG rules and populations.

## 16B. The exact macro object has two anonymous interfaces

Let `K` be the finite set of P/M port atoms, `Lambda` the label join-semilattice, `S` the score chain, `Q` the destination-atom set, and `B={0,1}` the stay flag.

A boundary carrier has two finite slot sets:

- `I`: exposed connectable port slots;
- `U`: persistent destination slots.

A row is

$$
r=(L,k,s,t,b)
\in
\Lambda\times K^I\times S\times Q^U\times\mathbb B.
$$

A complete exact macro is a finite relation

$$
R\subseteq\operatorname{Row}(I,U).
$$

Every row shares the same `I` and `U`. A primitive scalar destination value is a valuation on a singleton `U`; a composite destination tuple is a valuation on the disjoint union of parent destination slots.

For bijections `sigma:I->I'` and `theta:U->U'`, transport reindexes the row:

$$
(\sigma,\theta)_*(L,k,s,t,b)
=
(L,k\circ\sigma^{-1},s,t\circ\theta^{-1},b).
$$

The same pair acts simultaneously on **every row** of the relation. Exact behavioral equality is the orbit of the whole relation under this two-sorted action.

This corrects a subtle danger. A macro is not a set of independently sorted row bags. Different rows may encode correlations between the same shared slots. Sorting each row separately can erase those correlations.

## 16C. The destination field `T` is now formalized

Define the destination object on a finite set `U` by

$$
\mathcal T(U)=Q^U.
$$

For a bijection `theta:U->U'`, transport is

$$
\theta_*t=t\circ\theta^{-1}.
$$

For destination valuations `t in Q^U` and `u in Q^V`, output uses labelled-set coproduct

$$
t\sqcup u\in Q^{U\sqcup V}.
$$

It is natural:

$$
(\theta\sqcup\eta)_*(t\sqcup u)
=
\theta_*t\sqcup\eta_*u.
$$

The frozen implementation serializes this coproduct as

```text
dtup(T_left) + dtup(T_right).
```

The canonical isomorphism tests vary port and destination permutations independently and apply one destination permutation across all rows. A twelve-check destination audit and an eight-check frozen-core contract audit therefore supports the language-independent conclusion that `T` is an independently anonymous persistent destination interface. The frozen algebra does **not** currently contain an intrinsic map from a port slot to a destination slot.

A future change adding such incidence would define a new algebra and reopen the theorem.

## 16D. TreeMatch: the exact restricted wiring syntax

For input boxes `b` with port interfaces `I_b`, a **TreeMatch diagram** is a partial matching on the disjoint union of all half-ports such that:

1. each internal cable has exactly two endpoints;
2. those endpoints lie on distinct boxes;
3. no half-port is consumed twice;
4. the graph induced on boxes is connected and acyclic;
5. unmatched half-ports form the outer interface.

The one-box/no-edge diagram is the unit. Replacing a box by another TreeMatch diagram through a bijection to its exposed interface preserves pairwise matching. The new box graph is a tree: it is connected and has one fewer edge than vertices. Thus TreeMatch is a symmetric groupoid-coloured operad.

TreeMatch embeds faithfully into the established finite-set cospan syntax for undirected wiring. The cospan apex is the set of cables. Each matched pair maps to one internal cable; each unmatched half-port maps to its own boundary cable. The fibre structure recovers the original matching, so the map is faithful. Cospan substitution by pushout reproduces TreeMatch substitution.

![TreeMatch restricted wiring embedding.](figures/treematch_embedding.png)

This is a **restricted** embedding. General undirected wiring diagrams may contain multiway junctions, empty cables, copying, deletion, self-gluing, feedback, or trace. None is imported into frozen IG.

**Plain-language reading.** The legal IG composition trees really are a precise standard kind of wiring diagram, but a deliberately narrow one: only pairwise bridges arranged as a tree.

## 16E. Exact finite-relational semantics

For a two-sorted interface `(I,U)`, assign the finite relations

$$
\mathcal A(I,U)
=
\mathcal P_{fin}(\Lambda\times K^I\times S\times Q^U\times\mathbb B).
$$

Evaluate a TreeMatch diagram by:

1. choosing one row from every input relation;
2. imposing the frozen two-sided P/M bridge predicate on every internal cable;
3. hiding the consumed port slots;
4. keeping the values on unmatched outer slots;
5. joining labels, taking the minimum score, conjoining stay flags;
6. taking the coproduct of all destination valuations;
7. collecting and deduplicating all lawful output rows.

Nested and flattened evaluation enumerate the same lawful assignments. JOIN, MIN, AND, and destination coproduct are associative; existential elimination and set union are associative. Therefore this evaluation respects TreeMatch substitution and defines an algebra over TreeMatch.

# PART III - OPENING THE CLOSED OBJECT

## 17. Levels as ranks of realizability

Once exact substitution was closed, a new question became legitimate. A macro could safely replace a microscopic interior, but what kinds of organization does recursive composition actually create?

The generated objects were grouped into levels `L0, L1, L2, ...`. The word *level* can be misleading, so the interpretation must be fixed carefully.

A level is:

- a stage in recursive construction;
- a rank in the realizability program;
- a population of exact boundary relations and their inverse/factorization structures;
- an opportunity to ask which relational descriptions first become necessary or sufficient.

A level is not, by default:

- a time step;
- a physical layer of the universe;
- a scale of distance or energy;
- a spatial dimension;
- a causal era;
- a more actual state.

The most useful cross-level picture is a **realizability frontier**. As composition continues, the set of exact relations that can be realized changes. Sometimes the outer skeleton is new. Sometimes an old skeleton appears with a new multiplicity decoration. Sometimes the same visible relation is realized through a deeper organization. Sometimes an old operation becomes reusable in a richer setting.

There is no earned scalar law saying that every level is uniformly “more complex.” The frontier is vector-valued: support structure, multiplicity, incidence placement, overlap, ancestry, symmetry, local reconstructibility, and future response can change differently.

> **Plain-language reading.** The levels are not floors in a building or moments after a Big Bang. They are chapters in what the algebra is able to realize.

## 18. L0-L2: legality, encapsulation, factorization, and competition

### L0 - the local compositor

At L0 the central services are already present:

- bridge legality from selected-port `(P,M)`;
- consumption of the selected ports;
- retention of the remaining boundary;
- `L` by join;
- `s` by minimum;
- `T` from declared destination data;
- `stay` by conjunction;
- quotient by anonymous-interface isomorphism.

This is the exact local engine. It licenses composition and substitution but no authority, geometry, or physical interpretation.

### L1 - encapsulation and alternative interiors

L1 makes the consequence of exact closure visible. More than one microscopic realization may map to the same boundary class. The outside response is exact, but the inverse image can contain alternative parents or histories.

This creates two complementary facts:

1. **Forward encapsulation.** Future lawful contexts need only the boundary.
2. **Backward multiplicity.** The same boundary may have multiple exact microscopic realizations.

The second fact is not hidden state in the behavioral sense. It is inverse ancestry: information available when one deliberately asks how the boundary was generated, but irrelevant to future composition if the complete boundary relation is already known.

This separation later becomes a recurring theme: the forward quotient can forget distinctions that the inverse structure still retains.

### L2 - factorization and interchange

At L2 the algebra develops richer factorization structure. A composite can have multiple lawful decompositions and multiple saturated routes through its predecessor DAG. Disjoint attachments interchange up to interface isomorphism. Common-successor and alternative-route relations become meaningful.

Several important negative and positive facts appear.

#### Non-cancellativity

Different factors can lead to the same composite boundary. One cannot generally cancel a common context and recover a unique parent. This follows from explicit witnesses and is structurally consistent with forward encapsulation.

#### Nonunique factorization

A boundary may be reached through different decomposition routes. The algebra is not a unique factorization domain.

#### Simultaneous-demand dependence

Pairwise bridge possibilities do not automatically imply that several attachments can coexist. Different potential relations may compete for the same finite port capacity. This seed later becomes explicit in the inter-node program.

#### Interchange

For disjoint attachment sites, changing the order changes serialization but not the boundary isomorphism class. This is a real algebraic symmetry, not literal tuple equality.

### Early intervals

Comparable generated objects define finite intervals in the predecessor order. At rank gap two, the first interval family was trivial in the sense predicted by the basic order law. At rank gap three, non-isomorphic interval types appeared beyond simple cardinality summaries. A degree-signature invariant was sufficient in the frozen I1 analysis. These early interval results showed that organization lives in incidence, not merely counts.

## 19. L3-L5: overlap, possibility topology, and exact gluing

### L3 - maturation without a new primitive law

L3 largely transports and reorganizes the services already present at lower levels. Factorization, inverse ancestry, interval organization, and overlap become richer, but no uniquely new primitive compositor rule is earned.

This was an early warning against a naive “one new law per level” narrative. Recursive depth can generate new exact organization under an unchanged rulebook.

### L4 - higher-order overlap incidence

At L4, predecessor/support overlaps become rich enough to support nerves, nonflag incidence patterns, and homological signatures. These are exact combinatorial objects.

The word *topology* is appropriate in the mathematical sense: one can build a nerve from overlaps and compute invariants such as Betti numbers. But these are invariants of a possibility relation. They are not yet topology of physical space.

This distinction was made explicit after earlier temptation to read any cycle or hole geometrically. A homology class in a support nerve means that a family of exact algebraic overlaps has a certain combinatorial organization. Nothing yet gives length, dimension, locality, curvature, or embedding.

### L5 - exact support-identity gluing

L5 is one of the major steps in the internal program. It establishes an exact way to glue lower identities through shared support relations. The mechanism is not a scalar summary; it is an identity-level relational construction.

## 20. The S-series: local descriptions fail, relational completion succeeds

The S-series asked whether common-context reorganization could be explained by a local property of the selected port or selected attachment incidence. It followed a frozen ladder from cheap local summaries to complete relational structure.

Let `X<Y` be comparable exact objects and let a context `C`, through selected port `c`, attach lawfully to both endpoints. Let:

- `I = I(X,Y)` be the old exact interval;
- `X'` and `Y'` be the transformed endpoints;
- `I' = I(X',Y')` be the transformed interval;
- `K_c` be the exact action relation mapping old intermediates to every lawful canonical image under the same selected context port;
- `A = Im(K_c)` be the set of inherited images.

The reorganization observable partitions the transformed interval into

```text
H = I' intersect A       inherited states
N = I' minus A           new states
```

with signature

```text
(|N|, |I'|, |H|).
```

The counts are bookkeeping. The mechanism question is what exact relation determines the partition.

### L0: orbit cardinality fails

Port-orbit size was not predictive. Existing exact evidence contained 18,959 size-2-orbit zero-gain counterexamples and 7,144 size-1-orbit positive-gain counterexamples.

### L1: exact selected-port orbit is equivariant but not globally sufficient

Within a context, automorphic selected ports give the same canonical reorganization signature. This is theorem-level symmetry. Across unrelated contexts, however, orbit membership does not determine the result.

### L2: selected-incidence orbit fails

A stronger key containing the exact context, selected context-port orbit, and opposite selected-port `(P,M)` datum had an exact collision: identical L2 key, different reorganization signatures `(0,1,1)` and `(0,3,3)`.

### L3: complete local incidence type fails

Even the exact two-object local incidence type - exact carrier, carrier selected-port orbit, exact context, context selected-port orbit - failed on the preserved witness.

This exhausted the Local Sufficiency Gate. No further ladder of local descriptors was licensed.

### S1: old-interval action alone fails

The complete action relation `K_c` on every old intermediate still did not determine the new interval. An exact collision had the same `K_c` but signatures `(1,3,2)` and `(0,2,2)`.

### S2: the two-sided relational square succeeds

The complete object containing both the image relation and the transformed interval determines the result exactly. Once `A` and `I'` are known, the inherited/new partition is fixed.

The sufficient object can then be reduced:

```text
full square
  -> (K_c, I')
  -> (A, I')
  -> partition (H,N) of I'.
```

Explicit old interval identity is unnecessary for this observable because some old intermediates have no lawful image and therefore do not enter `A`. Source-to-image pairing is also unnecessary; only the exact image set matters.

### Final S-series conclusion

No new primitive selection mechanism was found. Common-context reorganization is generated by an inherited two-sided relation: which transformed states lie in the image of the old interval under the context action.

> **Plain-language reading.** Looking only at the socket being used was never enough. Even knowing exactly how the context acts on every old middle state was not enough. The answer appears only when that inherited image is compared with the complete new interval. The effect is relational, not a secret scalar carried by one port.

# PART IV - THE ALGEBRA FIGHTS BACK

## 22. Why falsification belongs in the center

A cleaned-up retrospective can make a research program look as though every concept was present from the beginning. That would be false here and scientifically damaging.

The Infinity Grid algebra became stronger because attractive explanations were allowed to fail. Several failures changed the mathematical object itself. Others narrowed a claim from general to restricted, from mechanism to recognition, or from theorem to bounded observation. Still others blocked premature moves toward geometry or actualization.

The falsifications are therefore not footnotes. They are part of the result.

A useful pattern appears repeatedly:

1. an economical summary works on a narrow panel;
2. the summary is proposed as a candidate mechanism;
3. a stronger, preregistered, or more diverse panel is opened;
4. an exact collision appears;
5. the claim is narrowed or rejected;
6. the next sufficient relational layer is sought.

This is how the algebra “fights back.” The researcher proposes a language; the exact possibility space decides whether that language is sufficient.

## 23. Serialization, provenance, and false state variables

**What survived:** exact interchange and substitution up to structure-preserving bijection.

**What died:** the assumption that tuple positions are physical or intrinsic.

### Correction 2: provenance was not the repair

**What survived:** inverse ancestry as a separately queryable relation.

**What died:** provenance as a required boundary state variable.

Several notions were at risk of being conflated:

- microscopic representative identity;
- hidden consumed-supply residue `C`;
- exact inverse ancestry;
- factorization certificate `K`;
- an actualization selector.

They are different. The first is quotiented from future behavior. `C` is a construction residue. `K` is a derived relation. Inverse ancestry records alternatives. None supplies authority to choose one actual continuation.

The mature sixteen-counter result prompted a tempting generalization: perhaps every row of an arbitrary macro relation could be independently converted to multiplicity counters and the set of row counters would classify the whole relation. Stage-2 integration killed that generalization exactly.


Every row in both relations has the same multiplicity profile: one `a` and one `b`. Independent row sorting therefore identifies the same row-counter type. # PART V - THE RELATIONAL MECHANISM PROGRAM, L6-L14

## 27. Scanner, Spectroscope, Local Sufficiency, and DRA

By L6, ad hoc exploration was no longer enough. The branch had accumulated too many exact relations, too many possible descriptors, and too many opportunities to mistake recognition for mechanism. Four instruments were standardized.

### 27.1 General Level Scanner

The Scanner asks what is exactly new at a level before attaching an interpretation.

A typical sequence is:

1. freeze a deterministic target or panel without reading the desired outcome;
2. reconstruct exact predecessor and support relations;
3. compare against complete lower populations where available;
4. classify first novelty by the existing A/B/C ladder;
5. enforce the Local Sufficiency Gate;
6. test minimality only if a nontrivial survivor remains;
7. examine cross-level reuse and forward necessity;
8. close or park every high-priority lane.

The Scanner is the discovery instrument. It is allowed to say that an exact relation is new, lower-realized, locally reconstructible, quotient-nonfaithful, or produced by an inherited operator. It is not allowed to call a cycle “space” or a recognition pattern “mechanism.”

### 27.2 Spectroscope

The Spectroscope asks whether an already-discovered exact relation is recognizable as a known mathematical structure: a graph class, incidence pattern, automorphism group, closure motif, homology signature, dominator relation, or similar object.

Recognition can improve language and suggest tests. It does not upgrade the causal or generative status of the relation.

The discipline is:

```text
Scanner discovers and reduces.
Spectroscope recognizes afterward.
```

A spectroscopic label must never be used to smuggle geometry or physics into a pre-geometric carrier.

### 27.3 Local Sufficiency Gate

Before claiming that a higher-order or global organization is necessary, the program tests a ladder of local descriptions. The exact ladder varies with the level, but the logic is fixed:

- `C0`: very cheap or unlabelled local structure;
- `C1`: one-carrier or one-support local profile;
- `C2`: pairwise overlap or shared-support profile;
- higher `Ck`: only if all lower local summaries fail.

If `C1` closes the distinction, it is scientifically wrong to advertise a global mechanism. If `C2` is necessary, `C1` must be falsified by an exact collision.

### 27.4 Decision Readiness Audit

The DRA prevents relational organization from being confused with selection or actualization. It asks whether alternatives, discriminants, forcing, persistence, endogenous authority, global compatibility, and global uniqueness are all present on a type-correct decision carrier.

A level can be rich enough to individuate carriers perfectly and still provide no authority to choose one continuation.

### 27.5 Evidence hierarchy

The ledger distinguishes:

- theorem/audit;
- exact finite result;
- theorem-derived consequence;
- restricted-family result;
- strong evidence without theorem;
- open or parked question.

This hierarchy is not bureaucratic decoration. It is the main defense against turning a beautiful pattern into a stronger claim than the evidence supports.

## 42. Capacity competition and the minimal edge carrier

### 42.2 Node-local capacity theorem


If endpoint channel types are not preselected, feasibility becomes a small finite capacity-constrained matching problem over the seven port types.

### 42.3 Why a relation cannot live inside unchanged endpoint states

## F.1 What the companion bundle contains

The proof bundle is curated rather than exhaustive and is deliberately kept below 30 MB. The full uploaded archive contains many nested duplicates, repeated large inputs, checkpoint populations, superseded artifacts, and caches. The companion **compact proof bundle** preserves the scientific chain while deduplicating heavy material. It includes:

- the authoritative v19.1 ledger plus compact machine-readable extracts;

- selected authoritative level documents/results needed for the claims in the manuscript;
- exact frozen specs, executed code, result files, theorem/audit texts, runtime/provenance, and manifests for the mature-node and inter-node claims;
- representative checkpoints where they materially establish chronology or state reconstruction;
- `SUPERSEDED_NUMERIC_QUANTITIES.csv`, preventing historical partition/slice counts from being silently promoted;
- claim/evidence and document/evidence maps;
- `OMITTED_HEAVY_DEPENDENCIES.csv` with source archive paths, sizes, and SHA-256 hashes for large inputs/checkpoint panels intentionally not duplicated.

Where a heavy run cannot be made fully standalone without exceeding the size target, the bundle is **proof-complete and dependency-complete by hash rather than byte-complete**: exact code/spec/results are present, and omitted large dependencies are named and hashed so they can be recovered from the preserved source archive without ambiguity.
- a complete manifest of the curated package.

The large source archives remain referenced by exact filename, logical nested path, size, and SHA-256 rather than duplicated unnecessarily.

## F.2 Reproduction philosophy

Each completed scientific step should contain, as applicable:

1. frozen question/specification;
2. exact executed code;
3. declared inputs and dependency hashes;
4. human-readable result;
5. machine-readable result;
6. runtime/platform information;
7. checkpoint/state information for heavy runs;
8. replay command and replay output;
9. SHA-256 manifest;
10. interpretation guard and non-claims.

## F.3 Heavy-run protocol

Long computations use the project protocol:

- separate warmup, load/cache, and official science;
- run a genuinely detached controller when foreground runtime is unsafe;
- checkpoint every deterministic stage or chunk;
- verify integrity before advancing;
- resume only missing or invalid chunks;
- reuse completed checkpoints;
- fail closed on any mismatch;
- preserve exact executed code and state.

The L14A3 handoff is an instructive example. A missing bucket artifact invalidated an old PASS marker. The correct response was surgical regeneration of that bucket, not blind trust and not rerunning all verified work.

## F.4 Replaying compact audits

Most selected packages contain a `README_FIRST.txt` and commands such as:

```text
python code/<audit>.py --root .
python code/<audit>.py --root . --reverse
```

Forward and reverse-order replays are used to detect accidental iteration-order dependence. Exact science digests are compared where provided.

## F.5 Evidence discipline

When reading a result, identify its tier:

- theorem;
- audited implementation premise;
- exact finite computation;
- bounded Scout observation;
- interpretation hypothesis;
- open question.

Do not upgrade a result because its case count is large. Do not downgrade a theorem because its proof is implemented computationally in one artifact. The claim/evidence map records the intended status.
