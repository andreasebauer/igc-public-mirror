SOURCE FINDINGS
infinity_grid/v05_validation_runtime.py::_run_group owns forked worker PIDs.
Its exceptional finally block sends SIGKILL to each pending worker and waits for
that worker only. Its deadline branch uses the same direct-worker-only pattern.
_child creates a separate selector through _run_node_process / subprocess.Popen.
_run_node_process calls communicate and publishes output on normal return, but
has no exceptional cleanup block for the selector process.
Worker and selector enable subreaper behavior. Their descendant waiting occurs
on successful paths; killing the worker bypasses its descendant wait entirely.
The controller then writes PAUSED and attempts a checkpoint after dispatch raises.

This ownership gap is consistent with V55's selector surviving controller return.
The code and observations establish missing cleanup coverage, not the mechanism
that eventually ended that selector. That mechanism remains unproven.

REPAIR REQUIREMENTS BEFORE DEV143 CAN BE QUALIFIED
1. Establish explicit ownership and readiness before a selector can start.
2. Route cancellation through a supervisor that remains alive until its selector
   and registered descendants have stopped and been reaped.
3. Handle cancellation during launch, communicate, result reduction and timeout.
4. Never treat a signal being sent, worker exit, or pipe EOF as proof that every
   descendant is gone. Preserve both the original error and any cleanup failure.
5. Use process identities in the correct PID namespace. Do not send signals to
   PIDs copied from the mounted /proc: V54 showed they differ from Python PIDs.
6. Do not signal unrelated processes, adopt broad system-wide cleanup, add a
   supervisor outside the native engine merely to make the acceptance test pass,
   or convert missing phases into a successful result.
7. Keep successful-run evidence publication, native save cadence/budgets, and
   immutable prior reports unchanged. Do not repair V55 evidence in place.
